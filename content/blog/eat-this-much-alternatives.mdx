Eat This Much plans meals to your calorie and macro targets. People look for an alternative for a few reasons: the plans come from its own database rather than food they already cook, weekly plans and the grocery list are on Premium, and its food data and prices are built around the US.

**Our pick is BiteSync.** It plans the week to your calorie and protein targets from *your* recipes, and the weekly planner and grocery list are free. It also checks your vitamins and minerals across the week.

*BiteSync is our app, so we're biased. That's why everything about the other apps below comes from their own websites, checked on the dates given under the table.*

## What to look for in an alternative

Decide first whether you want a **planner** or a **tracker**. Eat This Much is a planner: it decides the meals in advance so the day adds up before you eat. A tracker, such as Cronometer or MacroFactor, records what you've already eaten and tells you how close you came. If you liked that Eat This Much did the planning, look for another planner.

Then ask:

- **Whose food does it plan with?** Its own database, or recipes you choose?
- **Does it plan a whole week**, and is that free?
- **Does the shopping list come from the plan?**
- **Does it set your targets for you**, or do you have to work them out?

## 1. BiteSync: best for planning the week from your own food

- **Your recipes, not a database.** BiteSync builds the week from recipes you add, import from recipe websites by pasting the link, or (on Pro) have written for you. A plan made of meals you already like is one you'll stick to.
- **Weekly planner and grocery list on the free plan.** Each day's totals update as you place meals, so you can see a day is short on protein before you shop. The grocery list merges the whole week and leaves off what's already in your pantry.
- **Targets set for you, safely.** Your calorie and protein targets come from your weight, height, age, sex, activity and goal. BiteSync never goes below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men, and won't set a weight-loss target if your BMI is under 18.5.
- **Let it plan for you.** On Pro, the AI fills the whole week from your recipes in seconds. It aims for every day within 150 kcal of your calorie target, the week's average within 5%, and at least 95% of your protein, and tells you which days fall short instead of hiding it.
- **Vitamins and minerals across the week.** Pro checks 12, such as iron, iodine and vitamin D, against reference values split by sex, and steers the next plan towards whatever you're short on. [Why most meal planners can't do this](/blog/meal-planners-micronutrient-gaps).
- **UK food data.** Nutrition comes from the UK's official food composition tables (CoFID), and it's priced in pounds.
- **Built by a dietitian**, and it runs in any browser on phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install.

BiteSync is free to start. Pro is £6.99 a month or £59 a year and adds unlimited recipes, AI weekly plans and the full vitamin and mineral analysis. It doesn't scan barcodes.

## 2. Samsung Food: if you want a free recipe organiser

[Samsung Food](https://samsungfood.com/meal-planner/) saves recipes from the web, plans them by day or week, and makes a shopping list, all free. Personal nutrition targets and tailored full-week plans need Food+ ($6.99 a month or $59.99 a year in the US); free users get a generic daily calorie target. [BiteSync vs Samsung Food](/blog/bitesync-vs-samsung-food).

## 3. Cronometer: if you'd rather track every nutrient

[Cronometer](https://cronometer.com/gold/) is a detailed tracker covering up to 84 nutrients, with custom targets and a barcode scanner on its free plan. Gold costs $10.99 a month or $59.99 a year. It records what you've eaten but doesn't plan a week or make a shopping list, so it's a different kind of app from Eat This Much. [BiteSync vs Cronometer](/blog/bitesync-vs-cronometer).

## 4. MacroFactor: if you log everything and want targets that adjust

[MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactor/) estimates your calorie burn from your food log and weight trend, and adjusts your targets each week. It has no free plan ($11.99 a month or $71.99 a year after a 7-day trial), and its website doesn't describe a meal planner or a shopping list. [BiteSync vs MacroFactor](/blog/bitesync-vs-macrofactor).

## 5. Plan to Eat and Paprika: if you only need recipes on a calendar

[Plan to Eat](https://www.plantoeat.com/) ($5.95 a month or $49 a year after a 14-day trial) and [Paprika](https://www.paprikaapp.com/) (sold separately for each platform) save recipes from websites onto a meal plan and build a shopping list. You place the recipes yourself, and neither plans to calorie or protein targets.
