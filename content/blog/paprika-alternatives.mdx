## What changed in Paprika 4

Paprika used to be a one-off purchase for each platform. Paprika 4, released on 6 November 2026, is free to download everywhere, with three levels ([Paprika's pricing guide](https://www.paprikaapp.com/help/v4/ios/pricing/)):

| Version | Who gets it | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | New users | Up to 50 recipes, 1 grocery list, 1 cookbook, no cloud syncing |
| Standard | Anyone who bought an earlier version | Unlimited recipes, lists and cookbooks, syncing, advanced search, every platform |
| Premium | Subscribers | Everything, plus recipe scanning, social media import, barcode scanning, nutrition analysis, family accounts, calendar feeds and Alexa |

Premium launched at an introductory **$14.99 a year** with a 7-day free trial, or **$9.99 a year** for earlier buyers. Prices are in US dollars and vary by country ([Paprika 4 announcement](https://www.paprikaapp.com/news/)).

Paprika is clear that earlier buyers aren't forced to pay: "You are not required to subscribe to maintain access to Paprika." Standard users keep getting compatibility updates and bug fixes. The new nutrition analysis, which works out a nutrition label from a recipe's ingredients, is Premium only.

## 1. BiteSync: best for planning your week to your calories and protein

BiteSync is a weekly meal planner built by a dietitian. Where Paprika stores your recipes and lets you plan them, BiteSync starts from what you need to eat: it works out your targets, then helps you plan a week that meets them.

- **Targets set for you, with safety limits.** BiteSync works out your calorie and protein targets from your weight, height, age, sex, activity and goal. Your calorie target never goes below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men, and BiteSync won't set a weight-loss target if your BMI is under 18.5.
- **Your recipes, by link.** Paste a link from a recipe site, TikTok, Instagram or Facebook. BiteSync imports the ingredients and method, works out the nutrition, and you check everything before it's saved.
- **A week that adds up before you shop.** Place meals yourself and each day's calories and protein update as you go. Or let Pro fill the week from your recipes. It aims for every day within 150 kcal of your calorie target, the week's average within 5%, and at least 95% of your protein.
- **One grocery list.** It merges the whole week, groups items by type of food, and takes off what's already in your pantry.
- **Vitamins and minerals across the week.** Pro checks 12, including iron, calcium, vitamin D and folate, against UK reference intakes for your sex and age, and flags saturated fat, salt and free sugars.
- **Cook mode.** One step at a time, with timers, and your screen stays on.

**Price:** every new account gets a 7-day free trial of Pro, with no card needed. After that, Pro is £6.99 a month or £59 a year, billed in pounds wherever you live. There's no free plan for new accounts.

**Works on:** any web browser, on phone, tablet or computer.

**Not a recipe archive:** BiteSync doesn't import Paprika's export files, scan cookbook pages or track pantry expiry dates. Bring across the recipes you actually cook by pasting their links. To see how it handles your own recipes, read [the meal planner for your own recipes and macros](/blog/meal-planner-own-recipes-macros).

Targets from any app are a starting point. Speak to your GP or a dietitian before following a calorie target if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, are under 18, have or have had an eating disorder, have kidney disease, or take medicine for diabetes.

## 2. Mela: if you want to pay once, on Apple devices

[Mela](https://mela.recipes/) is the closest thing to old-style Paprika: a recipe manager you buy once. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and syncs through iCloud.

- **Saving recipes:** its in-app browser shows a live preview when it finds a recipe on a page, and a share extension saves recipes from other apps. It can also scan a recipe from a book, and subscribe to recipe blogs.
- **Planning and shopping:** meals go on a built-in calendar, stored in Apple's Calendar app so you can share the plan with people who don't have Mela. The grocery list lives in Apple's Reminders app.
- **Moving from Paprika:** Mela imports .paprikarecipes files, though it doesn't keep links to other recipes or images embedded in the text ([Mela help](https://mela.recipes/help)).
- **Privacy:** Mela says it doesn't collect any data.

**Price:** free to download, with a single one-off purchase that unlocks everything. Mela+ for iPhone and iPad is $6.99 on the US App Store ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mela-recipe-manager/id1548466041)), and the Mac version is a separate purchase.

**Drawback:** Apple only, and it doesn't plan your meals to calorie or protein targets.

## 3. Recipe Keeper: if you need Android or Windows

[Recipe Keeper](https://www.recipekeeperonline.com/) is a recipe organiser, shopping list and meal planner on iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows and Mac, with syncing between them.

- **Saving recipes:** import from hundreds of recipe websites, Instagram and TikTok, or scan recipes from books, photos and PDFs.
- **Planning and shopping:** weekly and monthly meal planners, and one step adds the whole plan to a shopping list grouped by aisle. You can keep a separate list for each store.
- **Moving from Paprika:** its App Store listing names Paprika among the apps it transfers recipes from.
- **Also:** printable cookbooks, an Alexa skill, offline access, and nutrition you can add to recipes and search by.

**Price:** free to download. The Pro Upgrade for iPhone and iPad is $19.99 on the US App Store, and other platforms are a separate purchase ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/recipe-keeper/id974683711)).

**Drawback:** paying for each platform adds up if you use several kinds of device.

## 4. Copy Me That: if you want the cheapest big collection

[Copy Me That](https://www.copymethat.com/) saves recipes from any website with one click, through a browser button or its app. Recipes link to a shopping list and meal planner: you make the list from your plan, it sorts items into categories, and you can keep several lists and a pantry list. It works in any browser and on iPhone and Android, and syncs across them.

**Price:** free, with every feature, for up to 40 recipes. Beyond that it's $1.00 a month, or a yearly or lifetime option, and one paid membership covers all your devices.

**Drawback:** it needs an internet connection.

## 5. AnyList: if the shared list matters most

[AnyList](https://www.anylist.com/features) is a shopping list first, with recipes and meal planning on top. The free app stores and shares recipes and lets you import up to 5 from websites.

**Price:** free. AnyList Complete adds unlimited web imports, recipe scaling and photos, a meal-planning calendar and a grocery list made from your plan, for $9.99 a year for one person or $14.99 a year for a household.

**Drawback:** planning and making a list from your plan both need Complete.

## 6. Plan to Eat: if you plan a big collection by hand

[Plan to Eat](https://www.plantoeat.com/) saves recipes from any website, social media or your own cookbooks. You plan the week on a calendar, and the shopping list fills itself, organised by aisle. One account covers your household on all your devices.

**Price:** no free plan. A 14-day free trial needs no payment details, then it's $5.95 a month or $49 a year ([Plan to Eat pricing](https://www.plantoeat.com/pricing)).

**Drawback:** it's a subscription, so it doesn't solve the problem if paying yearly is what put you off Paprika.

## When Paprika might still suit you best

If you already own Paprika, Standard is free and does most of what you used, so stay put unless something is missing. Paprika also still does things few others do in one app: a pantry that tracks amounts and expiry dates, and apps on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android and Windows under one account.
