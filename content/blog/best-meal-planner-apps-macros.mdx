## Planner or tracker? The question that matters most

Most "macro apps" are trackers. You log what you ate, and the app tells you how close you came. A planner works the other way round: you decide the meals in advance, and the app makes sure they add up before you've bought the food.

If you keep missing your protein target, a tracker tells you at the end of the day. A planner stops it happening. That's why we built BiteSync as a planner.

## 2. Eat This Much: if you want meals picked for you

[Eat This Much](https://www.eatthismuch.com/pricing) generates meal plans to your calorie and macro targets from its own recipe and food database. The free plan generates a day at a time. Premium ($5 a month, billed annually) adds weekly planning, an automatic grocery list and grocery delivery integration. It has barcode scanning and phone apps. Compared with BiteSync, the plans come from its database, not your own recipes, and weekly planning costs extra. [BiteSync vs Eat This Much](/blog/bitesync-vs-eat-this-much).

## 3. Cronometer: if you want to track every nutrient

[Cronometer](https://cronometer.com/gold/) is a detailed tracker. It covers up to 84 nutrients from a database of over a million lab-analysed or verified foods, with custom targets and a barcode scanner on its free plan. Gold costs $10.99 a month or $59.99 a year. It records what you've eaten but doesn't plan a week for you or make a shopping list. [BiteSync vs Cronometer](/blog/bitesync-vs-cronometer).

## 4. MacroFactor: if you log everything you eat

[MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactor/) estimates your calorie burn from your food log and weight trend, and adjusts your targets each week. It works best if you log consistently. It has no free plan ($11.99 a month or $71.99 a year after a 7-day trial), and its website doesn't describe a meal planner or a shopping list. [BiteSync vs MacroFactor](/blog/bitesync-vs-macrofactor).

## 5. Samsung Food: if you want a free recipe organiser

[Samsung Food](https://samsungfood.com/meal-planner/) saves recipes from the web, plans them by day or week, and makes a shopping list, all free. Personal nutrition targets and full-week tailored plans need Food+ ($6.99 a month or $59.99 a year in the US). Free users get a generic daily calorie target. [BiteSync vs Samsung Food](/blog/bitesync-vs-samsung-food).

## 6. MyFitnessPal: if you want to log everything you eat

[MyFitnessPal](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/premium) is the best-known food log. It has barcode and meal scanning on Premium and syncs with more than 35 apps and devices. Its Meal Planner is on Premium+, the top tier; in the US that's $24.99 a month or $99.99 a year. [BiteSync vs MyFitnessPal](/blog/bitesync-vs-myfitnesspal).
