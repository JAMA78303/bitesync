## Planner or tracker? The question that matters most

Most "macro apps" are trackers. You log what you ate, and the app tells you how close you came. A planner works the other way round: you decide the meals in advance, and the app makes sure they add up before you've bought the food.

If you keep missing your protein target, a tracker tells you at the end of the day. A planner stops it happening. Some people use one of each.

## Eat This Much: best for hands-off automatic plans

[Eat This Much](https://www.eatthismuch.com/pricing) generates meal plans to your calorie and macro targets from its own recipe and food database. The free plan generates a day at a time. Premium ($5 a month, billed annually) adds week-at-a-time planning, an automatic grocery list, grocery delivery integration and automatic leftovers. It also has barcode scanning and iPhone and Android apps.

**Less good if:** you'd rather eat your own recipes. The plans come from its database, not your favourite meals. [BiteSync vs Eat This Much](/blog/bitesync-vs-eat-this-much).

## BiteSync: best for fitting your own recipes to your targets

BiteSync is ours. You set your calorie and protein targets once, and on the free plan, each day's totals update as you place meals on the week. On Pro, the AI fills the whole week from your recipe library. It aims for every day within 150 kcal of your calorie target, the week's average within 5%, and at least 95% of your protein. If a day can't get there, it says which one. You get one grocery list for the week that leaves out what's already in your pantry, and you can import recipes from any recipe site by pasting the link. Pro also checks 12 vitamins and minerals, such as iron, iodine and vitamin D, across the whole week against reference values split by sex, and steers the next plan towards whatever you're short on.

**Less good if:** you want to log packaged food by barcode, track exercise, or use a native phone app. BiteSync is a web app with none of those. It also starts you with only 10 recipes, and it works out your targets from your stats; you can't type in your own. Free, or Pro at £6.99 a month or £59 a year, billed in pounds.

## Cronometer: best for detailed nutrient tracking

[Cronometer](https://cronometer.com/gold/) tracks up to 84 nutrients from a database of over a million lab-analysed or verified foods. That's the most detailed of any app here. The free plan is generous: custom macro and micronutrient targets, a barcode scanner, and syncing with other apps and devices. "Repeat Items" puts chosen foods or meals into your diary on set days. Gold ($10.99 a month or $59.99 a year) adds a recipe importer, different macro targets for each day of the week, and photo and voice logging.

**Less good if:** you want the week planned for you. Cronometer is a tracker; it doesn't build a week of meals or a shopping list. [BiteSync vs Cronometer](/blog/bitesync-vs-cronometer).

## MacroFactor: best for targets that adjust as you go

[MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactor/) estimates how many calories you actually burn from what you log and how your weight moves, then adjusts your macro targets every week. Logging is quick: search, barcode, nutrition-label scanning and AI-assisted logging. It also reports micronutrients.

**Less good if:** you want a free plan or a meal planner. There's a 7-day trial and then it's $11.99 a month, $47.99 for six months or $71.99 a year. Its website doesn't describe a meal planner or a shopping list. [BiteSync vs MacroFactor](/blog/bitesync-vs-macrofactor).

## Samsung Food: best free meal planner and recipe organiser

[Samsung Food](https://samsungfood.com/meal-planner/) lets you save recipes from the web and plan them by day or week. It turns the plan into a shopping list you can sort by aisle, and you can share it with family. It's all free on Android, iPhone and the web, and free users get three days of tailored meal ideas each week. Food+ ($6.99 a month or $59.99 a year in the US; prices vary by country) adds tailored plans for the whole week and personalised calorie, macro and micronutrient targets.

**Less good if:** you want precise macro planning without paying. Free users get a generic daily calorie target, and personal targets need Food+. [BiteSync vs Samsung Food](/blog/bitesync-vs-samsung-food).

## MyFitnessPal: best for logging everything you eat

[MyFitnessPal](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/premium) is the best-known food log. On Premium you get barcode and meal scanning and voice logging, and it syncs with more than 35 apps and devices. Its Meal Planner, with a grocery list, is on Premium+, the top tier. That's available in the US, UK, Canada, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand.

**Less good if:** planning is the main thing you want, because it sits on the most expensive tier. Prices vary by country: in the US, Premium+ is $24.99 a month or $99.99 a year. [BiteSync vs MyFitnessPal](/blog/bitesync-vs-myfitnesspal).

## How to choose

- **You want the app to decide what you eat:** Eat This Much, or Samsung Food+ if you'd also like recipes from across the web.
- **You cook your own favourites and want them fitted to your targets:** BiteSync.
- **You want to know every nutrient you ate:** Cronometer.
- **You want targets that adapt to your weight trend:** MacroFactor.
- **You want to log everything, with your watch synced:** MyFitnessPal.

These aren't exclusive. A common pairing is to plan the week in a planner and log the extras in a tracker.
