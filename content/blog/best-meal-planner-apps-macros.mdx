## Planner or tracker? The question that matters most

Most "macro apps" are trackers. You log what you ate, and the app tells you how close you came. A planner works the other way round: you decide the meals in advance, and the app makes sure they add up before you've bought the food.

If you keep missing your protein target, a tracker tells you at the end of the day. A planner stops it happening. That's why we built BiteSync as a planner.

Planning ahead has some research behind it too. In one of the largest studies on the subject, 40,554 French adults who planned their meals a few days ahead were more likely to eat a varied diet and to follow national nutrition guidelines closely ([Ducrot et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-017-0461-7)). It shows a link, not proof that planning causes the difference. [What the research says about meal planning](/blog/does-meal-planning-help).

## What to look for in a macro meal planner

1. **Targets you can trust.** Either the app works them out from your body and goal, with safety limits, or it lets you type in numbers someone else gave you. Be wary of any app that will set a very low calorie target without question.
2. **It plans the whole week.** A day at a time is fine for one day. Shopping, batch cooking and leftovers happen over a week.
3. **It uses food you'll actually eat.** A plan full of recipes you'd never cook is a plan you won't follow.
4. **Every day is checked, not just the average.** A week can average out on target while two days are far off.
5. **The shopping list comes from the plan.** Otherwise you're copying ingredients out by hand.
6. **It looks beyond macros.** Hitting your protein with the same few foods every day can leave gaps in iron, calcium or vitamin D.

## 1. BiteSync: best for planning meals to your macros

BiteSync is a weekly meal planner built by a dietitian. You plan the week from recipes you like, every day is sized to your calorie and protein targets, and the shopping list comes from that plan.

### Targets set for you, safely

BiteSync works out your calories from your weight, height, age, sex and activity, using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then adjusts for your goal: 20% below maintenance to lose weight, 10% above to build muscle. A percentage scales with you, unlike a flat 500 kcal.

Protein is set per kilogram of body weight, from 1.0 g to 2.0 g depending on how active you are and your goal, with an adjusted weight above a BMI of 30. Carbs and fat share the calories left after protein, with at least 0.6 g of fat per kilogram of body weight, and fibre is set at 30 g a day.

There are safety limits too. Your calorie target never goes below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men, and BiteSync won't set a weight-loss target if your BMI is under 18.5. Update your weight or goal and your targets recalculate, with no daily logging needed. Want to see your numbers first? The [free macro calculator](/macro-calculator) uses the same method, with no sign-up.

### A week that adds up before you shop

Place meals yourself and each day's calories and protein update as you go, so you can spot a short day before you shop. Or let Pro fill the week: the AI weekly plan fills breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks for all seven days from your recipes, in seconds. It aims for:

- every day within 150 kcal of your calorie target, or 7%, whichever is tighter
- the week's average within 5% of your calorie target
- at least 95% of your protein target every day

If your recipes can't get a day there, BiteSync tells you which days are off and by how much, rather than hiding it. Adding a lighter or snack-sized recipe usually fixes it. Vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and dairy-free planning are built in.

### Your own food

Plans are built from your recipe library, so it's food you'll actually eat. Paste a link from most recipe websites to import a recipe, type in your own, or, on Pro, have recipes written to fit your targets. BiteSync works out the nutrition ingredient by ingredient, by weight, using CoFID, the UK government's food composition tables. New accounts start with up to 16 recipes built to their targets, so the planner has something to work with on day one. [How planning from your own recipes works](/blog/meal-planner-own-recipes-macros).

### One shopping list

One tap turns the week into a single list. Repeated ingredients are merged, amounts come in things you can buy (a bulb of garlic rather than 3 cloves), what's already in your pantry is left off, and items are grouped roughly the way a supermarket is laid out. You can send what's left to buy to WhatsApp or Messages. [How the shopping list works](/blog/meal-planner-apps-shopping-list).

Pro checks 12 vitamins and minerals, such as iron, iodine and vitamin D, across the week you've planned, against UK reference intakes for your sex, and for iron your age. When you plan your next week, it leans the plan towards foods rich in whatever fell short. The Today screen shows your fibre against the 30 g target. Premium adds planning a week at a time, an automatic grocery list, grocery delivery integration, automatic leftovers and different settings for each day of the week. ## How to plan a week to your macros, in any app

1. **Start with your numbers.** You need a calorie target and a protein target. Carbs and fat can follow from those. Our [macro calculator](/macro-calculator) gives you all four, free.
2. **Choose recipes you already cook.** Six to ten is plenty for a week. Repeats are fine: cooking once and eating twice saves time and makes the numbers easier to hit. The Food Standards Agency advises eating leftovers within two days or freezing them ([FSA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely)).
3. **Build each meal around a protein food.** Your protein target is much easier to reach with some at breakfast, lunch and dinner than by trying to catch up at the end of the day. [How much protein you need](/blog/how-much-protein-do-you-need).
4. **Weigh food the way the recipe means it.** Rice, pasta and oats gain weight when cooked, so a dry weight logged as cooked, or the other way round, throws the numbers off. [Raw or cooked weight?](/blog/raw-or-cooked-weight)
5. **Check every day before you shop.** Look at each day's calories and protein, not just the week's average, then fix the short days by swapping a meal or adding a snack.
6. **Shop from one list.** Merge the week's ingredients and take off what you already have.

BiteSync does steps 1, 5 and 6 for you, and on Pro it can do most of step 2 as well.
