## What to look for in an app that writes your shopping list

Most meal planners can put a recipe's ingredients on a list. The ones worth paying for do more of the work:

1. **It's built from the whole plan**, not added one recipe at a time.
2. **Repeats are merged**, so two recipes that use onions become one line.
3. **Amounts are ones you can buy.** Nobody sells 2 tablespoons of olive oil.
4. **It knows what you already have**, so you don't come home with a third jar of cumin.
5. **It works in the shop**: grouped by aisle, quick to tick off, easy to read on a phone.
6. **It reaches your household**, as a shared list or one you can send.

Some apps also send the list to an online supermarket. That's handy where it works, but it depends on where you live.

## 1. BiteSync: best for a list that buys a week to your targets

BiteSync is a weekly meal planner built by a dietitian. You plan the week from recipes you like, every day is sized to your calorie and protein targets, and the shopping list comes from that plan.

### How the list is made

- **One tap from your plan.** In the planner, tap **Generate grocery list**, then choose which days and meals to shop for, so a midweek top-up can cover just Thursday to Sunday. When you plan next week from the Today screen, its list is made at the same time, and when you sign up, your first week is planned with its list ready.
- **Merged across the week.** The same ingredient from different recipes becomes one line, and a recipe you've planned twice is counted twice.
- **Amounts you can buy.** Spoonfuls and cups become millilitres, slices of bread become loaves, and garlic cloves become bulbs. Foods you buy whole, such as onions, peppers and eggs, show as a count, and common cupboard and dairy items show the pack to look for, such as "1 tin chickpeas (400 g needed)".
- **Your pantry is taken off.** Store-cupboard staples in your pantry are left off the list. If you've recorded how much of something you have, BiteSync takes that off the amount, and anything you've marked as running low stays on. Fresh food stays on unless you've recorded an amount, so you're not left short. When the list is ready, it tells you how many items it skipped or reduced.

### In the shop

- **Grouped by food group:** fruit and veg, protein and dairy, pulses, carbs, nuts and seeds, then the cupboard. That's roughly how a supermarket is laid out, and each group shows how many items are left.
- **Tick as you go.** A ticked item is crossed out where it is, so the list doesn't jump under your thumb, and a counter at the top shows how many you've ticked. Tick the last one and the list says "All done!"
- **Add or remove anything.** Add washing-up liquid or coffee by hand, or delete what you don't need.
- **Share what's left.** **Share list** sends the items still to buy, grouped as on screen, to Messages, WhatsApp, email or Notes on your phone, or copies them to paste anywhere.
- **Use what you bought.** Once items are in your basket, BiteSync can suggest more recipes from your library that use them.

### Why the plan behind the list matters

A list is only as good as the week it comes from. BiteSync works out your calorie and protein targets from your weight, height, age, sex, activity and goal, with safety limits: your calorie target never goes below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men, and BiteSync won't set a weight-loss target if your BMI is under 18.5. Want to see your numbers first? The [free macro calculator](/macro-calculator) uses the same method, with no sign-up.

As you place meals, each day's totals update. On Pro, the AI fills the week from your recipes in seconds. It aims for every day within 150 kcal of your calorie target (or 7%, whichever is tighter), the week's average within 5%, and at least 95% of your protein, and it tells you which days fall short rather than hiding it.

Planning ahead may do more than tidy up your shopping. In one of the largest studies on the subject, 40,554 French adults who planned their meals a few days ahead were more likely to eat a varied diet and to follow national nutrition guidelines closely ([Ducrot et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-017-0461-7)). It shows a link, not proof that planning causes the difference. [What the research says about meal planning](/blog/does-meal-planning-help).

**Price:** every new account gets a 7-day free trial of Pro, with no card needed, and making lists from your plan is part of it. After that, Pro is £6.99 a month or £59 a year, billed in pounds wherever you live. There's no free plan for new accounts: if your trial ends without upgrading, you can still tick off, add to, share and delete the list you have, but new lists from your plan need Pro.

**Works on:** any web browser, on phone, tablet or computer. You can add it to your home screen.

**What it doesn't do:** there's no grocery delivery or online ordering, no prices on items, and no live list your household edits together, because a shared list is a copy. You can't reorder the food groups to match your own shop. There's no barcode scanner, and BiteSync runs in your browser rather than as an App Store or Google Play app.

Targets from any app are a starting point. Speak to your GP or a dietitian before following a calorie target if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, are under 18, have or have had an eating disorder, have kidney disease, or take medicine for diabetes. BiteSync is for people aged 16 and over.

## 2. Plan to Eat: if you plan a big recipe collection by hand

[Plan to Eat](https://www.plantoeat.com/tour/automated-grocery-list-maker/) is a recipe organiser with a meal-planning calendar. Once you've placed recipes on your plan, it makes the shopping list for you. You can organise the list by store and aisle, and create and move categories to match the layout of your favourite store; it remembers your choices. A Staples List adds the things you always keep in stock in one go. You can send the list to nearby stores for delivery or pick-up, or text it to someone, and one account covers your household on as many devices as you like.

- **Best for:** home cooks with lots of saved recipes who want to plan by hand and shop in their store's order.
- **Price:** no free plan. A 14-day free trial needs no card, then it's $5.95 a month or $49 a year ([Plan to Eat pricing](https://www.plantoeat.com/pricing)).
- **Works on:** iPhone, Android and the web.
- **Drawback:** it shows nutrition facts for your recipes and daily totals on your planner, but its website doesn't describe targets, and you place every meal yourself.

## 3. Samsung Food: if you want a free list to share

[Samsung Food](https://samsungfood.com/grocery-list-app/) saves recipes from any website, plans your week and turns the plan into a shopping list, free. From the Planner, you tap **Add plan to shopping list** and choose the ingredients to add. The list combines ingredients, sorts by aisle or by recipe, and can be printed. You can collaborate on meal plans and shopping lists with family and friends, and in select countries you can turn the list into an online order.

- **Best for:** households that want a free planner and a list they can share.
- **Price:** free. Samsung Food+ adds AI meal plans tailored to you and removes ads, at $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year after a 7-day free trial ([Samsung Food meal planner](https://samsungfood.com/meal-planner/)).
- **Works on:** web and mobile apps, plus a Chrome extension.
- **Drawback:** its website doesn't mention a pantry, so you untick what you already have each time you add your plan to the list. Planning to your own targets? See [BiteSync vs Samsung Food](/blog/bitesync-vs-samsung-food).

## 4. AnyList: if the shared list matters most

[AnyList](https://www.anylist.com/features) is a shopping list first. Its free app shares lists with family and friends, sorts common grocery items into categories automatically, and lets you customise those categories to match your local store. Adding a recipe combines similar ingredients into a single item, and you can shop online at Walmart, Instacart, Kroger and more. The meal-planning calendar, and making a grocery list from your meal plan, need AnyList Complete, which also adds the web and Mac apps, item prices and store filters.

- **Best for:** households that mainly want one shared list, with a simple meal calendar for a small yearly fee.
- **Price:** free. Complete is $9.99 a year for one person or $14.99 a year for a household ([AnyList features](https://www.anylist.com/features)).
- **Works on:** iPhone and Android; web and Mac with Complete.
- **Drawback:** the list from your meal plan is on Complete only, and its features page doesn't mention nutrition.

## 5. Paprika: if you want a pantry that knows what you have

[Paprika](https://www.paprikaapp.com/) is a recipe manager with a meal planner, and you can add the whole week to your grocery list from it. The list combines matching items (2 eggs in one recipe and 3 in another become 5 eggs), sorts by aisle or by recipe, and can show only what's left to buy. Its pantry is the clever part: ingredients you have in stock are unticked for you when you add a recipe, unless the recipe needs more than you have or your stock is past its expiry date ([Paprika's grocery guide](https://www.paprikaapp.com/help/v4/ios/groceries/)).

Paprika 4, released on 6 November 2026, replaced Paprika 3's one-off purchases: the app is now free to download everywhere, and one subscription covers all your devices.

- **Best for:** cooks with a large recipe collection and a well-stocked kitchen.
- **Price:** free for up to 50 recipes and one grocery list, on one device. Premium launched at an introductory $14.99 a year, with a 7-day free trial, or $9.99 a year if you bought an earlier version; prices vary outside the US ([Paprika 4 announcement](https://www.paprikaapp.com/news/)). If you bought an earlier version, you also get its Standard plan free, with unlimited recipes, syncing and extra lists ([Paprika pricing](https://www.paprikaapp.com/help/v4/ios/pricing/)).
- **Works on:** iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android and Windows. There's no full web app; a read-only web viewer for Apple's HomeView is in beta.
- **Drawback:** no calorie or protein targets. Nutrition, on Premium, is calculated per recipe from USDA data, and family sharing is Premium too.

## 6. Prepear: if you want a free planner with Walmart ordering

[Prepear](https://www.prepear.com/) brings your recipes, meal plan and grocery list together, free. Save recipes from the web or add your own, put them on your plan, and build your grocery list from the meals you plan, adding everyday essentials. In the phone app, **Shop Now** sends the list to Walmart for pickup or delivery.

- **Best for:** Walmart shoppers who want a free planner and list.
- **Price:** free. Gold adds every meal plan and cookbook on Prepear and unlimited ad-free Cook Mode, with a 14-day free trial, then $119.99 a year ([Prepear Gold](https://www.prepear.com/prepear-gold/)).
- **Works on:** phone, tablet and computer, with iPhone and Android apps.
- **Drawback:** its website doesn't describe calorie or protein targets, or a pantry.

## 7. Eat This Much: if you want the meals picked for you too

[Eat This Much](https://www.eatthismuch.com/pricing/) generates meal plans to calorie and macro targets you set. The free plan plans a day at a time; the automatic grocery list is on Premium, which plans a week at a time and emails you the plan with a grocery list, matched to your targets, tastes, schedule and budget. You can scale meals up for extra people, which multiplies their ingredients on the list, and get your groceries delivered from Instacart, Amazon Fresh, Walmart and more.

- **Best for:** people who want an app to choose the meals as well as write the list.
- **Price:** free. Premium is $5 a month with an annual membership ($59.99 a year), or $14.99 month to month, with a 14-day free trial ([Eat This Much help](https://help.eatthismuch.com/help/what-do-i-get-for-subscribing)).
- **Drawback:** the grocery list and weekly planning are Premium only. How it compares on planning: [BiteSync vs Eat This Much](/blog/bitesync-vs-eat-this-much).

## When another app suits you better

If you shop with someone else and want one live list you both edit, AnyList and Samsung Food are built for that, and Plan to Eat shares one account across your household. If you want to order straight from the list, Eat This Much, AnyList, Prepear, Plan to Eat and Samsung Food link to online grocery shopping, depending on where you live. And if you'd rather arrange the list in your own shop's aisle order, AnyList and Plan to Eat let you.
