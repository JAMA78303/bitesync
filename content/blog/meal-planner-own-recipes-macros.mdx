**Yes: BiteSync is a weekly meal planner that builds your week from your own recipes and sizes it to your calorie and protein targets.** Paste a link from most recipe websites to import a recipe, or type in your own, and BiteSync works out the nutrition for you. It sets your targets for you and shows each day's totals as you plan. Other apps do part of this. Recipe organisers use your recipes but don't plan to targets. Macro planners plan to targets, usually with their own recipe databases in the mix. The closest alternative is Prospre, which plans to macro ranges you set from the recipes in your Recipe Book.

## How BiteSync does it

### 1. Get your targets

BiteSync works out your calories from your weight, height, age, sex and activity, using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then adjusts for your goal: 20% below for weight loss, 10% above for building muscle. Protein is set per kilogram of body weight, from 1.0 g to 2.0 g depending on how active you are and your goal, with an adjusted weight above a BMI of 30.

Carbs and fat follow from those two numbers. The calories left after protein are split between carbs and fat, with at least 0.6 g of fat per kilogram of body weight. You don't pick the split, and that's deliberate: calories drive weight change, protein protects muscle, and planning to two numbers leaves more of your recipes in play than planning to four.

There are safety limits too. Your calorie target never goes below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men, and BiteSync won't set a weight-loss target if your BMI is under 18.5. Want to see your numbers first? The [free macro calculator](/macro-calculator) uses the same method, with no sign-up.

Targets from any app are a starting point. Speak to your GP or a dietitian before following a calorie target if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, are under 18, have or have had an eating disorder, have kidney disease, or take medicine for diabetes. BiteSync is for people aged 16 and over.

### 2. Bring your recipes

- **Paste a link.** In Recipes, choose **Import from URL** and paste the recipe's address. BiteSync reads the ingredients and method from most recipe websites, but not all. Check the name and quantities before saving, especially cups and jar or packet sizes. If a page can't be read, you fill in the details yourself, and for TikTok, Instagram and Facebook posts it pulls what it can from the caption for you to check.
- **Or type your own.** Add the ingredients and amounts for the dishes you already cook.
- **Nutrition is worked out for you.** BiteSync calculates each recipe ingredient by ingredient, by weight, using CoFID, the UK government's food composition tables. An imported recipe uses the website's own figures when the site publishes them.

New accounts also start with up to 16 recipes built to their targets, so the planner has something to work with on day one. On Pro there's no limit on how many recipes you add.

### 3. Plan the week

Place meals yourself and each day's calories and protein update as you go, so you can spot a short day before you shop.

Or let Pro fill the week. The **AI weekly plan** button fills breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks for all seven days from your library, in seconds. It aims for:

- every day within 150 kcal of your calorie target, or 7%, whichever is tighter
- the week's average within 5% of your calorie target
- at least 95% of your protein target every day

If your recipes can't get a day there, BiteSync tells you which days are off and by how much, rather than hiding it. Adding a lighter or snack-sized recipe usually fixes it. Vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and dairy-free planning are built in.

On Pro, BiteSync also checks 12 vitamins and minerals across the week, such as iron, iodine and vitamin D, and a re-planned week leans towards foods rich in any that fall short. [How the vitamin and mineral check works](/blog/meal-planner-vitamin-mineral-checks).

### 4. Shop

One grocery list comes from the whole week. It merges repeated ingredients and leaves off what's already in your pantry.

**Price:** every new account gets a 7-day free trial of Pro, with no card needed, including up to 5 AI weekly plans; after those, the standard planner still fills your week. After the trial, Pro is £6.99 a month or £59 a year, billed in pounds wherever you live. There's no free plan for new accounts.

**Built for planning, not logging:** BiteSync plans the meals you'll cook rather than logging what you've eaten, so there's no barcode scanner. It runs in any browser on your phone, tablet or computer, rather than as an App Store or Google Play app.

## The other apps

### Eat This Much: meals picked for you

[Eat This Much](https://help.eatthismuch.com/help/how-to-add-custom-foods) generates meal plans to calorie and macro targets you set, from its own food and recipe database. You can add your own recipes, by hand or with its Import from URL option, and its help centre says the generator uses them "as long as they fit your nutrition targets and preferences". The free plan plans the current day's meals. Weekly plans and the grocery list are on Premium: $5 a month with an annual membership ($59.99 a year), or $14.99 month to month, with a 14-day free trial ([Eat This Much help](https://help.eatthismuch.com/help/what-do-i-get-for-subscribing)).

For this need, your recipes join the pool its generator picks from, alongside its own database, and planning a week at a time is paid. [The best Eat This Much alternatives](/blog/eat-this-much-alternatives).

**When Eat This Much suits you better:** if you'd rather have meals picked for you, or want different targets on different days, which Premium allows.

### Prospre: macro plans from your Recipe Book

[Prospre](https://www.prospre.io/) generates meal plans to your calorie and macro goals, and lets you set ranges for your macros. It only uses recipes in your Recipe Book, which can come from its database, its partner recipe developers or your own uploads. It makes a grocery list you can order through Instacart or Amazon Fresh. The free plan includes meal plans and grocery lists; Premium is $19.99 a month or $99.99 a year, and removes ads and adds an AI coach ([Prospre pricing](https://www.prospre.io/pricing)).

Compared with BiteSync, you set the macro ranges yourself, and its homepage and pricing page don't mention checking vitamins and minerals across your week.

**When Prospre suits you better:** if you follow your own macro split, such as keto, and want to set the ranges yourself.

### Samsung Food: a free recipe saver and planner

[Samsung Food](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planning/id1133637674) saves any recipe from any website, plans your week and turns it into a shopping list you can share with family, free. You can set daily nutrition targets free and see a macro breakdown of your planned meals. Food+ adds AI-personalised weekly meal plans tailored to your health goals, at $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year on the US App Store. Its listing doesn't say whether those plans are built from your saved recipes.

**When Samsung Food suits you better:** if you mainly want a free place to collect recipes and share a shopping list with your household.

### Plan to Eat: plan your own recipes by hand

[Plan to Eat](https://www.plantoeat.com/pricing) is a recipe organiser with a meal-planning calendar. You save recipes from any website with its browser clipper or your phone's share menu, drag them onto the planner, and it builds an aisle-by-aisle shopping list. It shows nutrition facts for your recipes and daily totals on your planner, but it doesn't make plans for you or set targets. There's no free plan: a 14-day free trial needs no card, then it's $5.95 a month or $49 a year.

**When Plan to Eat suits you better:** if you have a big recipe collection, want to plan it by hand, and don't need targets.
