Mealime is closing on **21 October 2026**. Its recipes are moving to Meals Hub, which lives inside the apps of Safeway, Albertsons, Vons and other US supermarket chains. If you shop at one of those, that's an easy move. If you don't, whether you're elsewhere in the US or in another country, you'll need a new app before the 21st. Which app suits you depends on what you used Mealime for: quick recipes and a tidy shopping list, or meals planned around a goal.

*BiteSync is our app, and it's one of the options below. We've tried to be fair about where something else would suit you better.*

## What's happening to Mealime

According to [Mealime's closing notice](https://www.mealime.com/closing):

- The app will be discontinued on **21 October 2026**.
- Mealime's recipes are moving to **Meals Hub**, part of the grocery apps run by Albertsons Companies: Safeway, Albertsons, Vons, Jewel-Osco and other US supermarket chains.
- Pro subscribers are no longer being charged, and Pro features are free for everyone until the app closes.
- Your account doesn't move across automatically. You'd sign up to an Albertsons Companies app and set your preferences again.
- All personal data will be deleted when the app shuts down. The notice doesn't mention any way to export your plans or saved recipes.

## Pick a replacement by what you used Mealime for

### You mainly wanted recipes and a tidy shopping list

A recipe manager is the closest like-for-like swap. Two well-established ones:

- **[Paprika](https://www.paprikaapp.com/)** keeps your recipes in one place and plans meals for a week or a month. Its grocery list is sorted by aisle and combines similar ingredients. It runs on iPhone, Android, Mac and Windows, and each version is sold separately.
- **[Plan to Eat](https://www.plantoeat.com/)** saves recipes from any website into your own collection. You drag them onto a meal-planning calendar, and the shopping list fills itself in. It works on iPhone, Android and the web, and costs $5.95 a month or $49 a year after a 14-day free trial.

With both of these, you choose the recipes and put them on the plan yourself. Neither builds the week around a calorie or protein target for you.

### You want the week planned around a goal

This is what BiteSync is for. Here's how it works:

- You set your calorie and protein targets once, worked out from your weight, activity level and goal.
- As you place meals on the week, each day's totals update, so you can see a day is short on protein before you shop, not at 9pm.
- One merged grocery list comes from the whole week, grouped by type of food. Anything you've marked as already in the cupboard is left off.
- On Pro, the AI fills the whole week from your recipes, sized to your targets. It also checks 12 vitamins and minerals, such as iron, iodine and vitamin D, across the week against published reference values.
- Nutrition is calculated from each recipe's ingredients using official food composition tables, so the numbers come from what's actually in the meal.

How it differs from Mealime:

- **Fewer recipes to start with.** Mealime came with a large library of photographed recipes. BiteSync starts you with ten recipes built to your targets. Your library grows from what you add, import or (on Pro) have written for you.
- **No hands-free cooking mode.** Mealime had one; BiteSync doesn't.
- **It's a web app.** It works in the browser on any phone or computer, with no App Store download.

BiteSync is free for up to 10 recipes. Pro is £6.99 a month or £59 a year, billed in pounds wherever you live, and adds unlimited recipes, AI weekly plans and the full micronutrient analysis.

## Keep the Mealime recipes you like

Mealime's recipes are also published as web pages. You can save any you want to keep while those pages are still up:

1. Open the recipe on mealime.com and copy the link.
2. In BiteSync, go to **Recipes**, tap **Import** (**Import from URL** on a computer) and paste it. The ingredients and method come across, and the nutrition is calculated from the ingredients.
3. Check the quantities before saving. The importer converts cups and pounds to grams, and packet sizes, such as a "4 oz pack" of feta, sometimes need correcting.

Paprika and Plan to Eat can both save recipes from websites too, so this works whichever app you choose. The free BiteSync plan holds 10 recipes. Pro has no limit.
