## What happened to Mealime

According to [Mealime's closing notice](https://www.mealime.com/closing):

- Mealime was discontinued on **21 October 2026**. Albertsons Companies had bought it in 2021.
- Its recipes moved to **Meals Hub**, inside Albertsons Companies' supermarket apps. Your account doesn't move across: you sign in to a store app and set some preferences again.
- Paid subscriptions were wound down, Pro features were made free for everyone before the end, and Pro subscribers weren't charged again.
- All personal data was to be deleted when the app shut down. Neither the notice nor Mealime's help centre mentions any way to export your recipes or plans.

When we checked on 30 October 2026, Mealime's App Store and Google Play listings were gone and its web app showed an "Offline for Maintenance" page. Its public recipe pages on mealime.com still loaded.

If you shop at an Albertsons Companies store, Meals Hub is the simplest home for Mealime's own recipes. For everyone else, here are the alternatives.

## 1. BiteSync: best for planning a week to your calories and protein

BiteSync is a weekly meal planner built by a dietitian. You plan the week from recipes you like, see every day's calories and protein as you go, and shop from one list.

- **Targets set for you, with safety limits.** BiteSync works out your calorie and protein targets from your weight, height, age, sex, activity and goal. Your calorie target never goes below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men, and BiteSync won't set a weight-loss target if your BMI is under 18.5.
- **A week that adds up before you shop.** Place meals yourself and each day's totals update, so you can spot a day short on protein before you buy the food. Or let Pro fill the week from your recipes in seconds. It aims for every day within 150 kcal of your calorie target, the week's average within 5%, and at least 95% of your protein.
- **Your recipes.** Paste a link from most recipe websites and BiteSync imports the ingredients and method and fills in the nutrition. New accounts also start with up to 16 recipes built to their targets.
- **One grocery list.** It merges the whole week, groups items by type of food, and takes off what's already in your pantry. On your phone you can share it to Messages, WhatsApp or email, or copy it to paste anywhere.
- **Vitamins and minerals across the week.** Pro checks 12: vitamins A, C, D and B12, folate, iron, calcium, zinc, magnesium, potassium, selenium and iodine. It compares them with UK reference intakes, which differ for men and women, and flags saturated fat, salt and free sugars. When you re-plan a week, it leans the new plan towards foods rich in any that fall short.
- **Cook mode, and diets built in.** Cook mode shows one step at a time, with timers, and keeps your screen on. Vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free and dairy-free planning are built in.

**Price:** every new account gets a 7-day free trial of Pro, with no card needed. After that, Pro is £6.99 a month or £59 a year, billed in pounds wherever you live. There's no free plan for new accounts: if your trial ends without paying, you can still see your recipes and finish that week, but planning new weeks needs Pro.

**Works on:** any web browser, on phone, tablet or computer. You can add it to your home screen.

**Built for planning, not logging:** it plans the meals you'll cook rather than logging what you've eaten, so there's no barcode scanner. It runs in your browser rather than as an App Store or Google Play app.

Targets from any app are a starting point. Speak to your GP or a dietitian before following a calorie target if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, are under 18, have or have had an eating disorder, have kidney disease, or take medicine for diabetes. BiteSync is for people aged 16 and over.

## 2. Eat This Much: if you want plans generated to exact macros

[Eat This Much](https://www.eatthismuch.com/) builds meal plans automatically to calorie and macro targets you set, from its own recipes plus any you add. The free plan makes one day of meals at a time and includes food tracking, barcode scanning and importing recipes from a link. Food pages show vitamins and minerals, but beyond calories and macros the only targets you can set are for fibre, sodium and cholesterol. It also has a page for people leaving Mealime.

- **Best for:** people who want meals picked for them to set calorie and macro numbers, including different targets on different days, such as carb cycling.
- **Price:** free. Premium is $5 a month with an annual membership ($59.99 a year) or $14.99 month to month, with a 14-day free trial ([Eat This Much help](https://help.eatthismuch.com/help/what-do-i-get-for-subscribing)). In-app prices on the US App Store differ.
- **Works on:** web, iPhone, iPad and Android. Its iPad app can also be installed on Macs with an M1 chip or later, though the App Store says it's "not verified for macOS".
- **Drawback:** whole-week plans and the grocery list are Premium only, so the free plan doesn't cover Mealime's plan-the-week-then-shop routine.

## 3. Samsung Food: if you want a free recipe saver with shared lists

[Samsung Food](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samsung-food-meal-planning/id1133637674) saves recipes from any website, plans your week and turns it into a shopping list you can share with family, free. Its App Store listing says groceries can be delivered from 23 retailers across 4 regions, and that over 218,500 of its recipes have nutrition information and health scores. Its release notes say nutrition goals have been free since September 2025: you set daily targets and get a macro breakdown of your planned meals. The paid Food+ tier adds AI weekly meal plans tailored to your health goals, and nutrition tracking with adaptive meal plans.

- **Best for:** households that want a free, all-round recipe saver and shared shopping list.
- **Price:** free. Food+ is $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year as in-app purchases on the US App Store.
- **Works on:** web, iPhone, iPad and Android. Its iPad app can also be installed on Macs with an M1 chip or later, though the App Store says it's "not verified for macOS".
- **Drawback:** the free app lets you set daily targets and see a macro breakdown of your planned meals, but AI weekly plans tailored to your health goals are on Food+ only, and its listing doesn't mention vitamins or minerals.

## 4. Prepear: if you want a free planner on web and phone

[Prepear](https://www.prepear.com/) saves recipes from the web with no limit, sorts them into cookbooks, and lets you plan a full week and make a grocery list from it, all free. You save recipes from your phone's browser with the share button. The phone app can send your list to Walmart for collection or delivery. Prepear Gold adds ready-made meal plans and cookbooks from recipe creators, and unlimited ad-free Cook Mode.

- **Best for:** people who want a free recipe box and weekly planner that works on phone and computer.
- **Price:** free. Gold has a 14-day free trial, then costs $119.99 a year, billed annually ([Prepear Gold](https://www.prepear.com/prepear-gold/)).
- **Works on:** web, iPhone, iPad and Android.
- **Drawback:** no calorie or protein targets. You place the recipes, and Prepear doesn't describe planning to nutrition.

## 5. Plan to Eat: if you want to plan your own recipes by hand

[Plan to Eat](https://www.plantoeat.com/) is a recipe organiser with a meal-planning calendar. You save recipes from any website with its browser clipper or your phone's share menu, drag them onto the planner, and it builds an aisle-by-aisle shopping list. It shows calories, protein, carbs and fat for each meal and day. It doesn't supply recipes or make plans for you, and one account covers your household.

- **Best for:** home cooks with a big recipe collection who want to plan by hand and get a tidy shopping list.
- **Price:** no free plan. A 14-day free trial needs no card, then it's $5.95 a month or $49 a year ([Plan to Eat pricing](https://www.plantoeat.com/pricing)). The US App Store lists a one-year membership at $54.99.
- **Works on:** web, iPhone, iPad and Android, with recipe clippers for Chrome, Firefox and Safari. Its iPad app can also be installed on Macs with an M1 chip or later, though the App Store says it's "not verified for macOS".
- **Drawback:** no targets, and no vitamins. Its nutrition covers calories, fat, saturated fat, cholesterol, sodium, carbs, fibre, sugar and protein, so sodium is the only mineral it shows.

## 6. Paprika: if you'd rather pay once

[Paprika](https://www.paprikaapp.com/) is a recipe manager. Its built-in browser downloads recipes from websites, and you plan meals on daily, weekly or monthly calendars. Grocery lists combine ingredients and sort them by aisle, and a pantry tracks what you have and when it expires. Paprika 4 has been in open beta since 28 September 2026, and its premium features will need a subscription after the beta.

- **Best for:** cooks building a large personal recipe collection who'd rather buy an app once than subscribe, at least while Paprika 3 is sold.
- **Price:** Paprika 3 is sold separately on each platform: $4.99 (£4.99 in the UK) on iPhone and iPad, and $29.99 on Mac or Windows. On Android it's free for up to 50 recipes, with in-app purchases. Paprika's website doesn't publish a Paprika 4 Premium price, but its App Store listing shows a "Paprika 4 Premium" in-app purchase at $14.99 (£14.99 in the UK), without saying how often it's billed.
- **Works on:** iPhone, iPad, Mac, Windows and Android. There's no web app.
- **Drawback:** no calorie or protein targets. Nutrition lives on each recipe only.

## 7. AnyList: if you mainly want a shared shopping list

[AnyList](https://www.anylist.com/features) is a shared grocery list first. Items are grouped automatically and sync across your household, free. AnyList Complete adds a meal-planning calendar that turns planned recipes into a list, unlimited recipe imports from websites (free accounts can import 5), and the Mac and web apps.

- **Best for:** households that mainly want a shared shopping list, with a simple recipe box and meal calendar for a low yearly fee.
- **Price:** free. Complete is $9.99 a year for one person or $14.99 a year for a household ([AnyList Complete](https://www.anylist.com/premium)), or £9.99 and £14.99 on the UK App Store.
- **Works on:** iPhone, iPad and Android, plus Mac, web and Apple Watch with Complete.
- **Drawback:** no nutrition features described. Its website, help centre and app listing don't mention calories, protein or vitamins.

## Moving your Mealime recipes

First, what you can't move. Your saved plans, favourites and settings went with the app: Mealime said all personal data would be deleted at shutdown, and its notice and help centre don't mention an export. Printing a recipe needed the web app, which is now offline.

What you can still do: Mealime's recipes are also public web pages on mealime.com, and they were still online on 30 October 2026. To keep one in BiteSync:

1. Open the recipe's page on mealime.com and copy the link.
2. In BiteSync, go to **Recipes**, tap **Import** (**Import from URL** on a computer) and paste the link.
3. BiteSync reads the ingredients and method, and works out the nutrition from the ingredients.
4. Check the recipe name and quantities before saving, especially cups and jar or packet sizes, then add the recipe to your week.

Mealime hasn't said how long these pages will stay up, so save the ones you love soon. The same importer works with most recipe websites. If a page can't be read, BiteSync asks you to fill in the details yourself, and for TikTok, Instagram and Facebook posts it pulls what it can from the caption for you to check.
