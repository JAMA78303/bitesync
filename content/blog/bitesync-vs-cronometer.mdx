Cronometer and BiteSync both take vitamins and minerals seriously, which is rare. They do it at different moments. Cronometer is a **tracker**: you log what you ate, and it reports up to 84 nutrients with real depth. BiteSync is a **planner**: you decide the week in advance, sized to your calorie and protein targets, and it checks 12 key vitamins and minerals across the week before you've shopped. If you want the fullest picture of what you ate, choose Cronometer. If you want the week built to hit your targets, choose BiteSync.

*BiteSync is our app. We've tried to be fair about where Cronometer is the better choice.*

## Where Cronometer is stronger

- **Depth.** It tracks up to 84 nutrients and other compounds, from a database of over a million lab-analysed or verified foods. BiteSync checks 12.
- **A generous free plan.** Free Cronometer includes custom macro *and* micronutrient targets, a barcode scanner, and syncing with other apps and devices. BiteSync's free plan covers calories and protein; all macros and the micronutrient analysis are on Pro.
- **Logging anything.** Barcode scanning on free, and photo and voice logging on Gold, make packaged and restaurant food easy to record. BiteSync has no barcode scanner.
- **Native apps.** Cronometer has phone apps. BiteSync is a web app that runs in your browser.

## Where BiteSync is stronger

- **It plans the week.** Cronometer can put chosen foods into your diary on set days ("Repeat Items"), but it doesn't build a week of meals to your targets. BiteSync Pro fills the whole week from your recipes. It aims for every day within 150 kcal of your calorie target and at least 95% of your protein, and says which days fall short. On the free plan, each day's totals update as you place meals.
- **A shopping list.** One merged list comes from the week's plan and leaves out what's already in your pantry. Cronometer doesn't make shopping lists.
- **Gaps fixed, not just reported.** BiteSync judges vitamins and minerals across the whole week, which is how adequacy works. When the week is short on something, say iron or iodine, the next AI plan favours recipes rich in it. The 12 include the ones that most often fall short, such as iron, iodine, vitamin D and B12. [Why the weekly view matters](/blog/meal-planners-micronutrient-gaps).
- **Recipe import on the free plan.** Paste a link from any recipe site. In Cronometer, the recipe importer is a Gold feature.

## Using both

They fit together well. Plan the week in BiteSync so the meals add up and the shopping is done in one trip. Then log in Cronometer if you want every nutrient of what you actually ate, snacks and meals out included.

- **Choose Cronometer** if you want the most detailed record of what you eat, with a free plan that covers micronutrient targets and barcode scanning.
