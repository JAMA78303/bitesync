**This 7-day vegetarian meal plan averages 118 g of protein and 1,748 kcal a day, and every day reaches its 112 g protein target.** It gets there with eggs, dairy, tofu, tempeh, Quorn, lentils and beans, and no protein powder. Each meal gives about 30–40 g of protein, with a smaller snack on top.

It's built for a 30-year-old woman, 165 cm and 70 kg, who exercises 3–5 times a week and wants to lose weight slowly. BiteSync's free [macro calculator](/macro-calculator) gives her 1,761 kcal and 112 g of protein a day. A 30-year-old man, 178 cm and 80 kg, who exercises as much and wants to keep his weight steady gets the same 112 g of protein, but 2,740 kcal, so he needs bigger portions: see Scaling it to your numbers, below.

## How the plan was built

- **Every number is calculated, not estimated.** We ran each recipe through BiteSync's own nutrition code, which uses CoFID, the UK government's food composition tables. Hummus, chia seeds, smoked paprika and ground cumin aren't matched to CoFID in BiteSync, so they use BiteSync's own food list.
- **Each day sits within BiteSync's target band.** That's within 123 kcal of 1,761 for this target (7%, or 150 kcal if that's tighter), with at least 95% of the protein target, which is 106 g.
- **Batch cooking does a lot of the work.** The dal covers three lunches, and the chilli and the bolognese each cover a dinner and the next day's lunch.
- **Weights are as you'd weigh them.** Rice, quinoa, pasta, noodles, oats and red lentils are dry weights. Tinned beans and chickpeas are drained. [Why raw and cooked weights matter](/blog/raw-or-cooked-weight).

## Batch cooking and food safety

- **Sunday or Monday:** make three portions of the dal. Keep Monday's in the fridge and freeze the other two for Wednesday and Saturday.
- **Monday:** make two portions of the chilli: one for dinner, one for Tuesday's lunch.
- **Thursday:** make two portions of the bolognese: one for dinner, one for Friday's lunch.
- **Everything else is cooked fresh on the day,** including the salad on Thursday and Sunday.

The Food Standards Agency advises eating leftovers within two days or freezing them, and defrosting in the fridge, then eating within 24 hours ([FSA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely/how-to-chill-freeze-and-defrost-food-safely)). Rice needs more care. Cook it fresh if you can. Otherwise cool it within an hour, keep it in the fridge for no more than a day, and reheat it only once, until it's steaming hot ([FSA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/home-food-fact-checker/home-food-fact-checker)). [How planning cuts food waste](/blog/meal-planning-reduce-food-waste).

## The recipes

Amounts are for one serving. Multiply them for the batch-cooked meals.

### Quark, raspberry and oat pot

*419 kcal · 38 g protein · 7 g fibre*

- 200 g quark
- 40 g porridge oats
- 80 g raspberries (frozen is fine)
- 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds
- 1 tsp honey

Stir the oats into the quark the night before so they soften. In the morning, top with the raspberries, seeds and honey.

### Eggs and cottage cheese on toast with spinach

*519 kcal · 36 g protein · 5 g fibre*

- 2 eggs
- 2 slices wholemeal bread
- 50 g baby spinach
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 120 g cottage cheese

Wilt the spinach in the oil, then scramble or poach the eggs. Spread the toast with cottage cheese and pile the spinach and eggs on top.

### Protein overnight oats

*478 kcal · 30 g protein · 10 g fibre*

- 50 g porridge oats
- 200 ml semi-skimmed milk
- 100 g quark
- 1 tbsp chia seeds
- ½ banana

Mix the oats, milk, quark and chia in a jar and leave in the fridge overnight. Slice the banana on top in the morning.

### Red lentil and spinach dal with brown rice and a quark raita

*546 kcal · 39 g protein · 15 g fibre*

- 60 g dried red lentils
- 50 g onion
- 100 g tinned tomatoes
- 40 g baby spinach
- 1 garlic clove
- 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp garam masala
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 50 g brown rice (dry weight)
- 100 g quark
- 40 g cucumber

Soften the onion, garlic and ginger in the oil, then add the spices for a minute. Add the rinsed lentils, tomatoes and about 250 ml of water per portion. Simmer for 20–25 minutes until thick, then stir in the spinach. For the raita, mix the quark with grated cucumber. Serve with the rice.

### Chickpea, edamame and feta quinoa salad

*579 kcal · 33 g protein · 19 g fibre*

- 120 g tinned chickpeas, drained
- 80 g frozen edamame (shelled soya beans)
- 50 g quinoa (dry weight)
- 30 g feta
- 60 g cucumber
- 80 g cherry tomatoes
- 60 g red pepper
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp lemon juice

Cook the quinoa, adding the edamame for the last 3 minutes, then drain and cool. Toss with the chickpeas, chopped vegetables, oil and lemon juice, and crumble the feta over.

### Quorn and bean chilli with brown rice

*562 kcal · 35 g protein · 22 g fibre*

- 100 g Quorn mince
- 80 g tinned kidney beans, drained
- 150 g tinned tomatoes
- 50 g onion
- 80 g red pepper
- 1 garlic clove
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 60 g brown rice (dry weight)
- 20 g cheddar

Soften the onion, pepper and garlic in the oil, then stir in the spices. Add the Quorn, beans and tomatoes and simmer for 15 minutes. Serve over the rice with the cheddar grated on top.

### Tofu and edamame noodle stir-fry

*563 kcal · 33 g protein · 11 g fibre*

- 150 g firm tofu
- 80 g frozen edamame (shelled soya beans)
- 60 g dried egg noodles
- 100 g pak choi
- 80 g red pepper
- 20 g spring onions
- 1 garlic clove
- 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tsp olive oil

Press the tofu dry, cube it and fry in half the oil until golden, then set aside. Stir-fry the vegetables, garlic and ginger in the rest of the oil, then add the cooked noodles, edamame, tofu and soy sauce.

### Paneer, chickpea and spinach curry with basmati rice

*665 kcal · 35 g protein · 10 g fibre*

- 80 g paneer
- 80 g tinned chickpeas, drained
- 120 g tinned tomatoes
- 50 g baby spinach
- 50 g onion
- 1 tbsp Indian curry paste, such as tikka or balti
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 50 g basmati rice (dry weight)

Brown the cubed paneer in the oil and set aside. Soften the onion in the same pan, stir in the curry paste, then add the tomatoes and chickpeas and simmer for 10 minutes. Return the paneer, stir in the spinach until it wilts, and serve with the rice.

### Smoky tempeh, sweet potato and broccoli traybake

*518 kcal · 37 g protein · 20 g fibre*

- 120 g tempeh
- 200 g sweet potato
- 150 g broccoli
- 50 g onion
- 2 tsp olive oil
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 30 g hummus

Toss the cubed sweet potato and onion wedges with the oil and paprika and roast at 200°C (180°C fan) for 15 minutes. Add the sliced tempeh and broccoli and roast for 15–20 minutes more. Serve with the hummus.

### Lentil and Quorn bolognese with wholewheat pasta

*537 kcal · 32 g protein · 23 g fibre*

- 70 g wholewheat pasta (dry weight)
- 30 g dried red lentils
- 60 g Quorn mince
- 150 g tinned tomatoes
- 80 g mushrooms
- 50 g onion
- 50 g carrot
- 1 garlic clove
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 15 g cheddar

Soften the chopped onion, carrot, mushrooms and garlic in the oil. Add the lentils, Quorn, tomatoes and about 150 ml of water per portion, and simmer for 20 minutes until the lentils are soft. Serve over the pasta with the cheddar.

### Chickpea shakshuka with feta and toast

*558 kcal · 35 g protein · 15 g fibre*

- 2 eggs
- 80 g tinned chickpeas, drained
- 200 g tinned tomatoes
- 80 g red pepper
- 50 g onion
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 30 g feta
- 1 slice wholemeal bread

Soften the onion and pepper in the oil, add the spices, then the tomatoes and chickpeas, and simmer for 10 minutes. Make two hollows, crack in the eggs, cover and cook for 6–8 minutes until the whites are set. Crumble the feta over and serve with the toast.

## Shopping notes

- **Check the cheese is vegetarian.** Some cheddars and fetas are made with animal rennet, so look for one labelled suitable for vegetarians.
- **Check the curry paste.** Thai curry pastes often contain shrimp paste or fish sauce. The plan uses an Indian paste, but check the label whichever you buy.
- **Quark** is a soft, high-protein cheese sold near the yoghurts. Skyr is the closest swap but has a little less protein.
- **Tofu varies.** The plan uses CoFID's figure for steamed tofu, 8 g of protein per 100 g. Many firm tofus in shops list more, so your days may come out a little higher.
- **The plan contains** milk, eggs, soya, gluten, peanuts and sesame (in the hummus). Swap foods you're allergic to, and check the new numbers.

## Nutrients to watch

Cutting out meat and fish makes a few nutrients harder to get ([NHS: the vegetarian diet](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/how-to-eat-a-balanced-diet/the-vegetarian-diet/)). We ran the whole week through BiteSync's weekly vitamin and mineral check. These are the daily averages against UK reference intakes for women aged 19–49:

| Nutrient | This plan | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Iron | 17.9 mg | 14.8 mg |
| Calcium | 1,100 mg | 700 mg |
| Zinc | 15.3 mg | 7 mg |
| Vitamin B12 | 3.3 µg | 1.5 µg |
| Iodine | 150 µg | 140 µg |
| Vitamin D | 1.5 µg | 10 µg |

*The garam masala, curry paste and lemon juice have no vitamin and mineral data in BiteSync's table, so the real totals are very slightly higher.*

- **Iron.** Iron from plants is harder for your body to absorb, which is why it helps to aim above 14.8 mg. Including vitamin C in the same meal helps ([NHS: iron](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/iron/)). The tomatoes, peppers, spinach and lemon in these recipes provide it. Men and women over 50 need 8.7 mg a day.
- **Vitamin B12** is found naturally only in animal foods. Here, eggs, milk, quark and cottage cheese provide most of it.
- **Iodine** is only a little over 140 µg, and almost all of it comes from milk, quark, cottage cheese and eggs. If you swap to a plant milk, choose one fortified with iodine: not all are ([BDA: iodine](https://www.bda.uk.com/resource/iodine.html)).
- **Vitamin D** stays low on food alone, as it does for most people. The NHS advises everyone to consider a daily 10 microgram supplement in autumn and winter, and some people all year ([NHS: vitamin D](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/)).
- **Omega-3 fats.** Without oily fish, the NHS suggests linseed (flaxseed) oil, rapeseed oil, walnuts, seeds such as linseed and chia, and eggs enriched with omega-3.

## Scaling it to your numbers

**Need more calories?** The 80 kg man above needs about 1,000 kcal more, but no more protein. Bigger portions of rice, pasta, oats and bread, an extra snack and a handful of nuts get him there.

**Need fewer?** Leaving out the snack takes off 130–200 kcal a day and leaves roughly 100–112 g of protein. Don't go lower than the calculator suggests. BiteSync never sets a target below 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 kcal for men.

**Want more protein?** Swap the banana snack for quark and berries, or add 100 g of edamame to a lunch, which adds about 12 g.

For your own calorie and protein targets, use the [macro calculator](/macro-calculator). [How much protein you need](/blog/how-much-protein-do-you-need) explains the ranges.

## Making it vegan

Swap the quark and cottage cheese for soya yoghurt, the eggs for tofu, the paneer for extra tofu or tempeh, and the milk for a fortified soya drink. Most Quorn contains egg, but there's a vegan range. Soya yoghurt has much less protein than quark, so add tofu, tempeh or edamame to keep protein up.

On a vegan diet, vitamin B12 has to come from fortified foods or a supplement, and iodine needs the same care ([NHS: the vegan diet](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/how-to-eat-a-balanced-diet/the-vegan-diet/)).

## When to get individual advice

Vegetarian diets suit people at every stage of life when they're well planned ([Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025)). This plan's calories and protein are for one example adult, though. Speak to your GP or a dietitian before following it if:

- you're pregnant or breastfeeding;
- you have kidney disease, or have been told your kidney function is reduced;
- you have, or have had, an eating disorder;
- you take medicine for diabetes;
- you're under 16.
