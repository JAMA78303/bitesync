MyFitnessPal and BiteSync solve different halves of the same problem. MyFitnessPal is a **food log**: you record what you ate, scanning barcodes as you go, and it adds up the day. BiteSync is a **meal planner**: you decide the week in advance, sized to your calorie and protein targets, and get one shopping list for it. If you want to log everything you eat, including packaged snacks and exercise, MyFitnessPal is the stronger tool. If you want to know before you shop that the week will hit your targets, that's what BiteSync is built for.

*BiteSync is our app. We've tried to be fair about where MyFitnessPal is the better choice.*

## The core difference: before or after

Logging and planning answer different questions.

A log answers *"what did I eat?"*. It's accurate, but it only tells you after the fact. The familiar result is finding out at 9pm that you're 40 g short on protein, with nothing left to do about it.

A plan answers *"what will I eat?"*. You see the day's numbers before you've bought the food, so you can fix them while there's still time: swap a lunch, add a yoghurt, move the curry to Thursday. The trade-off is that a plan only knows what you planned. When the day doesn't go to plan, you change it to match what you actually ate. BiteSync's Today screen records meals you ate, skipped or swapped, so the numbers stay real.

## Where MyFitnessPal is stronger

- **Logging anything.** Barcode scanning and meal scanning (Premium) make it quick to log packaged and branded foods. BiteSync has no barcode scanner. Its nutrition is calculated from ingredients using official food composition tables, so it's built for meals you cook, not for logging a packaged sandwich.
- **Exercise and devices.** MyFitnessPal tracks exercise and syncs with more than 35 apps and devices. BiteSync does neither.
- **Native apps.** MyFitnessPal has iPhone and Android apps. BiteSync is a web app that runs in your phone's browser.

## Where BiteSync is stronger

- **Planning is the main job, and it's free.** The weekly planner and the grocery list are on the free plan. MyFitnessPal's Meal Planner needs **Premium+**, its top tier.
- **Nutrition from what's in the meal.** Recipes are calculated ingredient by ingredient using official food composition tables (the UK's CoFID), unless an imported recipe's website publishes its own figures. Basic foods like chicken, lentils and oats have the same nutrients wherever you shop.
- **Vitamins and minerals across the week.** MyFitnessPal's help centre lists vitamin A, vitamin C, calcium, iron and potassium. BiteSync Pro checks 12 vitamins and minerals, including iodine, vitamin D, B12, folate and zinc, against published reference values, split by sex. It looks at the whole week, not each day, and when your week is short, the planner favours recipes that close the gap. [Why most apps can't show this](/blog/meal-planners-micronutrient-gaps).
- **Price.** BiteSync Pro is £6.99 a month or £59 a year, billed in pounds wherever you live. MyFitnessPal's prices vary by country, and its Meal Planner is on Premium+, the tier above Premium. In the US, Premium+ is $24.99 a month or $99.99 a year. In the UK, Premium alone is £15.99 a month or £64.99 a year.

## Can you use both?

Yes, and some people do: they plan the week in BiteSync, then log the extras in MyFitnessPal, like the coffee-shop latte and the snacks at work. If you only want one app, choose by the question you most want answered. "What did I eat?" points to MyFitnessPal. "What should I eat this week to hit my targets?" points to BiteSync.
